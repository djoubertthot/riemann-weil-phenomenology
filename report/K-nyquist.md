Nyquist spacing = π/τ = 2π/L.

| μ | Nyq | K = #{gaps > Nyq} | extra length |
|---|-----|-------------------|--------------|
| 6 | 3.51 | 1 | 0.02 |
| 11 | 2.62 | 1 | 0.90 |
| 14 | 2.38 | 2 | 1.29 |
| 22 | 2.03 | 6 | 2.71 |
| 38 | 1.73 | 12 | 5.50 |
| 50 | 1.61 | 15 | 7.18 |

The only large gap is 3.52 (between γ1 and γ2).
The desert 1.79 is *shorter than Nyquist* until
L is large. At μ=11 Lemma 2 used the wrong
piece: the sub-Nyquist hole is (γ1,γ2), not
[0,γ1].

K grows with μ because Nyquist shrinks and
ordinary gaps cross the threshold. That is
the extra exponent in ell(L): more pieces
in the Landau–Widom union, not a deeper
single desert.
