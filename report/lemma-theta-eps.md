# ε of the bump is not a truncated Gaussian

    ε  =  v₀ + √2 ∑_{n≥1} vₙ  =  √L ψ(0)

A Gaussian with the observed curvature,
sampled on N hats with the ONB ratio
vₙ/v₀ = (−1)ⁿ √2 exp(−α n²), α=π²/(−ln λ),
gives a truncation defect ε_G ∼ last term
(10⁻² at four modes).

Q's actual eigenmode at the same dimension:

| dim | ε_Q | ε_G | C_bump |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8.0×10⁻⁶ | −4.5×10⁻² | 0.112 |
| 5 | 3.9×10⁻⁷ | 7.2×10⁻³ | 0.123 |
| 6 | 6.4×10⁻⁸ | −7.4×10⁻⁴ | 0.075 |

ε_Q is 10³–10⁴ times smaller than ε_G.
Q spends the extra hats on killing the
edge, not on matching more Gaussian
coefficients. That is the Dirichlet wall
in v-space: the ground state is the
mid-bump closest to {ε=0}.

λ and ε² fall together, so C = Lλ/ε²
stays O(0.1). Controlling C is controlling
that joint rate, not summing a theta
series.
